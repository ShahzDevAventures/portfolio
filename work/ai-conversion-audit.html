01Evidence trail

68% churned "data-rich, insight-poor."

User interviews and telemetry on Sirge's standard analytics tool said the same thing from two directions: 68% of SMB e-commerce merchants churned, and the reason they gave was that the dashboards showed them everything and told them nothing.

Store owners could see ROAS. They could not see their conversion rate dropping, and when they could, they had no CRO expertise to work out why or what to change. The alternative was a $5,000+/month agency retainer.

02Problem statement
Shopify merchants under $1M ARR lack in-house CRO expertise, so they waste ad spend on leaking funnels because raw analytics dashboards never give them explicit execution steps.
03Tradeoffs

A rigid task list, not an AI chat.

Chose

Structured, task-oriented checklist

Over

Open-ended conversational AI

Gave up

Flexibility. A merchant can only do what the list contains.

Got

Completion. Merchants preferred explicit instructions to writing prompts; task completion ran 4.2× higher than the chat prototype.

Vision AI at 60% of a strategist's depth.

Chose

A vision-AI model scoring design, copy and mobile UX

Over

Human consulting per store

Gave up

About 40% of the audit depth a human strategist produces.

Got

100× the scale, and a report that arrives instantly instead of in two weeks.

04How we arrived at the solution

Card-sorting with 30 store owners, then heuristics mapped to one-click fixes.

We ran card-sorting exercises with 30 store owners to find the CRO quick wins that mattered most to them and that they could actually execute.

Each platform heuristic was mapped directly to a single-click fix: high cart abandonment auto-prompts Shop Pay and Apple Pay setup, for example. Visual-AI page parsing then pinpointed mobile layout errors and copy gaps automatically, so the list was specific to the store, not generic advice.

05What happened

Merchants who acted stayed.

The +38% lift in 90-day retention came specifically from merchants who executed at least three recommended tasks during onboarding. The product's value was in what it got people to do, not in what it showed them.
