1. The home page is slow to show on phones

High impact Conversion rate
Current state
On phones the main image takes 6.1 seconds to appear (Largest Contentful Paint, or LCP: the time until the biggest thing on screen shows). The hero image is 3,800 pixels wide but phones show it at about 400. A chat app and a quiz app load before the page appears.
Industry standard
Google counts LCP of 2.5 seconds or less as good. Competitor A and Competitor B both show their main image in under 2.5 seconds on phones (PageSpeed Insights, same day).
Why it matters
Visitors leave before they see the coffee. In a 2020 Deloitte study for Google, a mobile site 0.1 seconds faster went with 8.4% more retail conversions.
Recommended approach
Send phones a phone-sized hero image. Load the chat and quiz apps after the page has shown. Test again in PageSpeed Insights.
A/B test
Hypothesis: a faster home page raises phone conversion rate. Split 50/50 between the current theme and a faster copy, for example with Shopify Rollouts. Run 3 weeks. Watch phone conversion rate.
Success metrics
LCP at or under 2.5 seconds in PageSpeed Insights real-user data within 28 days. Phone conversion rate up against the merchant's current rate.
Evidence

PageSpeed Insights, mobile, https://example-store.com/, real-user data for the 28 days to 3 November 2026: LCP 6.1 s, INP 180 ms, CLS 0.04. Hero image file width read from the page source.

2. The hero headline does not say what the store sells

High impact Conversion rate Quick win
Current state
The hero says "Crafted with passion". It does not say the store sells coffee, who it is for or why it is different. The review count (4.8 stars from 2,140 reviews) only appears on product pages.
Industry standard
Competitor A's hero reads "Fresh-roasted coffee, at your door every two weeks", with its review count under the button.
Why it matters
Visitors decide in a few seconds whether they are in the right place. A vague headline sends them back to search.
Recommended approach
Rewrite the headline to name the product, the customer and the benefit. Put the star rating and review count under the main button.
A/B test
Hypothesis: a headline that names the product raises clicks from the home page to products. Split 50/50, 2 weeks. Watch home page to product page click rate, then conversion rate.
Success metrics
Click rate from the home page to a product or collection up 10% or more (target, set by this audit).
Evidence

Home page hero text and product page review widget, https://example-store.com/, loaded 4 November 2026.

3. The free-shipping threshold is only in the announcement bar

Medium impact Average order value
Current state
The announcement bar says "Free shipping over $40". Product pages and the cart page do not mention it. Most bags cost $18, so one bag leaves a customer $22 short.
Industry standard
Competitor B shows how far the customer is from free shipping in the cart and next to the price.
Why it matters
A visible threshold gives people a reason to add a second bag. In Baymard Institute's survey, 40% of US shoppers who left a checkout said extra costs such as shipping were too high.
Recommended approach
Show free-shipping progress in the cart. Repeat the threshold next to the price on product pages.
A/B test
Optional for a medium issue. Hypothesis: a progress message raises average order value. Split 50/50, 3 weeks. Watch average order value.
Success metrics
More orders over $40 and a higher average order value in Shopify Analytics.
Evidence

Announcement bar on every page; empty cart page https://example-store.com/cart; two product pages. All loaded 4 November 2026. Claude did not add anything to the cart.
